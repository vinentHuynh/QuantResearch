MNQ overnight block — exit research

Everything tested against the validated 18:00→06:00 ET long. Four exit-side ideas were put up against the clock and all four lost; one generalisation question about overnight drift was answered yes-but. This page is the record, with the charts and the numbers each conclusion rests on.

2. Giveback anatomy — how big is the prize? ceiling is real

Before testing any exit rule, measure what a better exit could theoretically capture: the distance from the night's own high to where the block actually closes.

Metric (index points)MeanMedianp90p99Max
Giveback (high − close)66.642.8150348885
MFE (high − entry)73.850.8——755
Realised (close − entry)7.311.2——710
  • A typical night closes 43 points ($86/micro) below its own high; the mean gap is 67 points. The block collects ~$15/night of it.
  • Capture ratio (realised ÷ MFE) has a median of 0.26 — a quarter of the run is kept. Only 9% of nights close in the top 10% of their own range.
  • The high is bimodal: 18% of nights top out in the 18:00 hour, 19% in the 05:00 hour, with a trough around 23:00–00:00. There is no mid-night hour to wait for.
  • Replicates out of sample: NQ 2015–2019 median giveback 0.201% of entry vs MNQ 0.265%, capture median 0.26 in both eras.

Why this is a ceiling, not an opportunity: the high is found by looking backwards. Live, you never know it was the high until it is gone. Sections 3–5 test whether any forward-looking rule reaches it.

  • Out of sample agrees at every level (NQ baseline $16.66 → $13.41 / $12.31 / $12.18).

5. Earlier exit clocks — 00:00 and 01:00 rejected

Same 18:00 entry, only the exit clock changes. Wall-clock exits (DST-correct), filled at the boundary bar's open. Motivated by the drift map: 23:00–02:00 carries the bias while 03:00–05:00 carries nothing.

Out of sample agrees: NQ 00:00 $6.70, 01:00 $8.46, base $15.58 (pair-t −1.95 / −1.61). Halving the night costs 56–60% of the edge and buys nothing — Sharpe falls to 0.55, PF falls, MaxDD is flat to worse. Only the single worst night improves, which is just less exposure time.

The decisive number

Price the leg you would be walking away from, on its own:

  • 00:00 → 06:00: +$7.78/night, t +2.06, 54.8% positive (OOS +$8.88, t +1.95)
  • 01:00 → 06:00: +$7.35/night, t +2.10 (OOS +$7.12, t +1.61)

The second half of the night is a standalone edge with its own significance — about half the block's entire P&L. It is not dead time.

No lucky hour exists

Scanning every exit hour from 21:00 to 06:00, both eras rise near-monotonically toward 06:00 (in sample: 21:00 $5.88 → 03:00 $9.95 → 06:00 $13.23). The one interior bump — OOS 03:00 at $14.91 — is contradicted in sample ($9.95, pair-t −1.12), so it is noise, not a window.

6. Does the overnight session trend long generally? yes, but weakly

Asked because if overnight drift is a general bias, it contaminates or subsidises any other strategy holding more than an hour overnight. Measured on every clock hour of the day, in basis points so the eras compare.

SampleMean per hourtHours observed
MNQ overnight (18:00–06:00)+0.33 bps+2.2420,454
NQ overnight, OOS 2015–2019+0.30 bps+2.4515,048
MNQ RTH (09:00–16:00)+0.48 bps1.16 (n.s.)11,564
NQ RTH, OOS+0.17 bps0.50 (n.s.)8,700
  • Overnight is not bigger drift than RTH — it is more consistent drift, because overnight hourly vol is roughly half (sd 11–29 bps vs 26–52 bps).
  • Per trade it is nearly invisible. Drift ÷ that same trade's own standard deviation: 1h hold 1.6% (win rate 51.8%), 4h 2.9% (53.4%), 12h 4.3% (54.5%). A single overnight long is ~97% noise.
  • Not evenly spread. Replicating in both eras: 23:00–02:00 starts on 3–6h holds, and 8–12h holds from 18:00–20:00. Dead or negative: 21:00–22:00 and 03:00–05:00.
  • MNQ's 11:00 RTH cell (t 2.4) fails to replicate in NQ (t 0.3) — the multiple-testing noise you would expect from 161 grid cells.
  • Decay: 2020–2022 +0.42 bps/h → 2023–2026 +0.26 bps/h. Halved, still positive.
Practical consequences
  • Longs get a tailwind about the size of costs (~$1/hour per micro; a 4h overnight long carries ~$4 of drift against $0.50 round trip plus slippage). It can flip a marginal strategy positive; it cannot carry one.
  • Overnight shorts pay it permanently. Fade and mean-reversion strategies held more than an hour overnight start behind by that amount every night.
  • Benchmark contamination. Any strategy that ends up long inside 18:00–06:00 will show "edge" that may be pure drift. Score against buy-the-window, not against zero.

7. What survives

The 12-hour window stays exactly as it is. Four exit-side ideas were tested — fixed targets, trailing stops, arm-delayed trailing stops, and earlier exit clocks — and all four lost to the plain 06:00 exit in both eras, with pair-t between −1.6 and −2.8. Combined with the earlier finding that tight sigma stops bleed edge while only the 2σ disaster stop is neutral, the pattern is consistent and mechanical:

Every rule that shortens exposure sells the compounding. The block's P&L is a slow drift that whipsaws constantly on the way. Any exit that reacts to a pullback, caps an advance, or truncates the clock is selling the thing that pays.

The giveback measured in §2 is real, large, and — on this evidence — not harvestable by any pullback-reactive rule. That is what you would expect if the window is a drift/risk-premium effect rather than a swing to be traded.

Still untested

  • Entry-side conditioning rather than exit-side — the one degree of freedom this session did not attack.
  • Position sizing by prevailing vol, which changes the risk profile without touching the clock.
  • Other products: the drift generalisation was measured only on NQ/MNQ. CL and GC 5-min data is cached locally and untested for this. Gold was separately found to be shape-similar but not significant (pooled t 1.71), with a carry trap at the session seam.

8. Method, caveats, how to rerun

Conventions used everywhere above

  • Costs in ticks, never bps — $0.50 round trip per MNQ micro, charged in every arm including the baseline, plus one extra tick of slippage on any stop fill.
  • Paired t-statistics on identical nights, so market noise cancels and only the rule's effect is measured. An unpaired comparison of two exit rules is nearly meaningless at these effect sizes.
  • Pessimistic fills: limits require a tick through the level (queue risk); stops gap-through fill at the bar open; trailing levels never use the same bar's high.
  • Two eras always. NQ 2015–2019 is out of sample, with dollar thresholds scaled to the same percentage move and P&L rescaled to today's notional — a $200 target on NQ at 4,400 is not the same trade as on MNQ at 24,000.
  • Nights missing bars (holidays, early closes) are dropped from every arm together.

Caveats that bound all of it

  • The full sample 2015–2026 contains no multi-year bear market. This is an equity-index overnight risk premium measured across a period that flattered it; a sustained downtrend is the untested case.
  • Baseline reads $14.03/night in §3–4 and $13.23 in §5. The §5 run drops nights missing any scanned exit bar and fills at the 06:00 bar's open rather than the 05:55 close — same edge, slightly stricter fill.
  • Losses cluster in vol events (Mar-2020, Aug-2024, Apr-2025) and are not filterable from prior-day features.
